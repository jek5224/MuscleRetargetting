# Ideas — 2026-03-25: Multi-Fiber LOD Hierarchy

## Fiber Grouping Pipeline

1. Bake 100 fibers per muscle across diverse motions
2. For each fiber at each baked frame, compute `dl/dtheta` (moment arm per DOF)
3. Most muscles affect 1-2 joints → each fiber's fingerprint is 1-2 scalar curves
4. Build agglomerative hierarchical clustering (dendrogram) per muscle from moment arm trajectory similarity
5. Dendrogram reveals: effective fiber count, redundancy, structural boundaries

## Force Application — Every Anchor Matters

From `Muscle.cpp`: `ApplyForceToBody()` applies segment-direction forces at **every** anchor/waypoint, not just origin/insertion. `GetJacobianTranspose()` computes Jacobians for all anchors. The full polyline path determines mechanical effect — two fibers with same endpoints but different interior paths genuinely differ.

## Grouping Criterion

Moment arm trajectory similarity: for each fiber pair, RMS difference of `dl/dtheta` curves across baked poses. Hierarchical clustering on this distance → dendrogram.

## Representative Fiber Placement

When fibers merge, representative sits at the group's centroid in (u,v) space — between original sample positions. MVC computes waypoints at any continuous (u,v), so representatives aren't constrained to the grid.

## Fiber Count Per Muscle

No need to decide upfront. Oversample (100 per muscle), build dendrograms. Dendrogram jump heights reveal how many groups each muscle actually needs. Total budget set by RL cost; each muscle's dendrogram determines its share.

## Torque Band Visualization

X-axis: frame or joint angle. Y-axis: moment arm for one DOF. 100 curves per muscle form a band. Narrow band = uniform = few groups. Wide/splitting band = diverse = many groups.

## 3D Dendrogram Visualization

Fiber (u,v) positions on XY plane, linkage height on Z axis. Low links = redundant, high links = distinct. Color by cluster at chosen K.

## Force-Length as Additional Grouping Dimension

From `Muscle.cpp`: `l_mt0` (rest length) = sum of segment lengths. Different fiber paths → different rest lengths → different force-length curves. Two fibers with identical moment arms but different rest lengths produce peak force at different joint angles — they're not truly redundant. Grouping should account for both `dl/dtheta` AND `l/l_m0` trajectories. Both are directly computable from baked waypoint positions (fiber length at each frame = sum of segment lengths).

## Transfer Learning Across LOD Levels

The dendrogram gives natural policy warm-start: when splitting cluster C into children C1, C2, initialize both children's activation to C's learned activation. The policy already knows the right ballpark — it just needs to learn the differential.

## Minimum Fiber Count From Dendrogram

Large jump heights in the dendrogram (especially where moment arm sign changes) are hard structural boundaries. The number of clusters below the first large jump = minimum viable fiber count for that muscle. This is a quantitative, per-muscle answer derived from data.

## Tendon Regions in Volume Simulation

Currently the volume sim treats the entire muscle as uniform soft body — all parts can shrink/extend equally. But muscles like tensor fascia lata are mostly tendon, which should be much stiffer.

**Problem**: The sim doesn't distinguish tendon vs muscle belly. Hill-type models encode tendon properties implicitly (series elastic element), but the tet sim allows arbitrary deformation everywhere.

**Possible approach**: Mark contour regions as tendon vs muscle (e.g., from anatomy — origin/insertion ends are typically tendinous). Apply stiffness constraints to tendon regions:
- Energy-based: add a strain-limiting penalty to tendon tetrahedra so they resist stretching
- Material property: assign higher Young's modulus to tendon-region tets
- Visual: color tendon regions white for verification

**Impact on line muscle**: Waypoints passing through tendon regions would deform less than those in the muscle belly. This naturally produces:
- Non-uniform length change along the fiber (belly shortens more, tendon stays ~constant)
- Different effective force at each waypoint — tendon-region anchors transmit force more rigidly, muscle-region anchors have more compliance
- More realistic force-length behavior without explicit Hill tendon modeling

**Open question**: Should waypoints in tendon regions exert force differently on the skeleton? In a series connection, force is the same throughout (basic mechanics). But the force-length/velocity modulation should only apply to the muscle belly portion. Knowing which waypoints are in tendon vs belly gives a better `l_m` estimate from the sim — belly-region segment lengths sum to true muscle length, tendon-region segments sum to tendon length. This feeds a more accurate `g_al(l_m/l_m0)` without changing force application per anchor.

### References: Tendon vs Muscle in FEM Literature

**Simplest approach — same constitutive model, different stiffness:**
- Blemker, Pinsky & Delp (2005), "3D model of muscle reveals causes of nonuniform strains in biceps brachii," J. Biomechanics. Uses transversely isotropic hyperelastic for both muscle and tendon; tendon gets higher shear moduli (G1, G2).

**Separate constitutive models:**
- Knaus, Handsfield & Blemker (2021), "3D Model of Soleus reveals effects of aponeuroses morphology," J. Biomechanics. Muscle = transversely isotropic hyperelastic with active/passive; aponeurosis = Mooney-Rivlin + exponential fibers (500 MPa modulus vs much lower for muscle). Most detailed tendon/muscle material separation.
- Hodgson et al. (2012), "FE modeling of passive material influence on skeletal muscle," J. Mech. Behav. Biomed. Materials. Tendon uses isotropic cubic hyperelastic; muscle uses transversely isotropic hyperelastic.
- Spyrou & Aravas (2011), "Muscle and Tendon Tissues: Constitutive Modeling," ASME J. Applied Mechanics. Unified framework: both fiber-reinforced composites, but muscle fibers depend on strain+rate+activation, tendon fibers depend on strain only.

**Heterogeneous stiffness solver:**
- Modi et al. (2021), "EMU: Efficient Muscle Simulation in Deformation Space," Computer Graphics Forum. Specifically designed for stiffness disparity (muscle << tendon << bone) in one mesh. Relevant if solver has numerical issues with large stiffness ratios.

**Graphics approaches:**
- Teran, Blemker et al. (2003/2005) — FVM for muscle simulation with transversely isotropic hyperelastic constitutive model. Foundational framework.
- Fan, Litven & Pai (2014), "Active Volumetric Musculoskeletal Systems," SIGGRAPH. Muscles = active rods with contractile stress; tendons = passive elastic rods.

**Practical takeaway**: Simplest path is Blemker's approach — mark tendon-region tets, assign higher stiffness, keep same solver. No architectural change needed.
