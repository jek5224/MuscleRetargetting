# Ideas — 2026-03-19: Multi-Fiber Muscle Model for RL

## Context
Exploring advantages of multi-fiber muscle models over single-line (or 2-3 line) models, specifically for muscle-based physics human model reinforcement learning.

## Multi-Fiber Advantages for RL

- **Richer action space**: Independent (or smoothly coupled) fiber activations give the policy access to within-muscle recruitment patterns — e.g., anterior vs posterior deltoid producing different torque directions
- **Smoother torque landscape**: Multiple independently wrapping fibers produce smooth aggregate torque, avoiding moment arm discontinuities that single-line models create. Better gradients for RL.
- **Reduced muscle neglect**: Single-line RL tends to find sparse solutions where some muscles (e.g., gluteus minimus) go unused. Multi-fiber with spatial coupling makes this harder.
- **More accurate force-length behavior**: Different fibers at different lengths → blended, broader force-length curve

## Activation Coupling: Mechanical Similarity, Not Spatial Smoothness

**Problem**: Fully independent per-fiber activations explode the action space. Shared activation per muscle loses functional diversity for wide-span muscles (deltoid, trapezius).

**Initial idea was spatial smoothness** — nearby fibers have correlated activations. But this is wrong:
- Cylinder muscle, 4 similar fibers: `[1, 0, 0, 0]` is bad → smoothness should prevent this ✓
- Deltoid, 3 functionally different fibers: `[0, 0, 1]` is correct → spatial smoothness would wrongly penalize this ✗

**Revised insight**: The criterion is not spatial proximity but **mechanical similarity**. "Smooth" = fibers with similar moment arm profiles have similar activations. Fibers with different moment arm profiles are free to differ arbitrarily.

**Why the LOD hierarchy already solves this**: The moment arm clustering defines which fibers are mechanically redundant. At any hierarchy level:
- Within a cluster: one shared activation (fibers are mechanically similar by construction)
- Across clusters: fully independent (fibers are mechanically distinct)

No separate smoothness penalty or hyperparameter needed. The clustering threshold that defines hierarchy levels IS the smoothness — and it's determined by physics (moment arm divergence), not by tuning.

**Refinement — smoothness is resolution-dependent, not a fixed rule**:
The acceptable activation discontinuity between neighboring fibers scales with spatial distance between them:
- 3 fibers (sparse): `[0, 0, 1]` — jump of 1.0 over a large distance → visually and physically fine
- 20 fibers (dense): `[0,...,0,0,0,1]` — jump of 1.0 between millimeters-apart fibers → jarring and implausible
- 20 fibers (correct): `[0, ..., 0.2, 0.4, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0]` — smooth gradient → natural

This is not an added constraint but a consequence of what dense fibers represent — nearby points in a muscle genuinely behave similarly. The underlying activation field has a natural bandwidth (spatial frequency), determined by the muscle's mechanical complexity (number of functionally distinct regions). Fiber count is the sampling resolution of that field; control point count is the field's complexity. As long as control points ≤ fibers, the result looks natural at any resolution. The LOD hierarchy handles this automatically: same continuous activation field sampled at different densities produces resolution-appropriate smoothness.

**Intermediate cases**: For a muscle with a gradient of behavior (e.g., 4 fibers with activations `[0, 0.5, 0.5, 1]`), the hierarchy provides natural resolution:
- 1 cluster → `[0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5]` (fully coupled)
- 2 clusters → `[0.25, 0.25, 0.75, 0.75]` (load balanced within pairs)
- 4 clusters → `[0, 0.5, 0.5, 1]` (unconstrained)

The operating LOD level determines the effective coupling. Action space = number of clusters, not number of fibers.

**Final simplification — fiber count IS the smoothness**:
The separate concepts of smoothness, grouping, and control resolution collapse into one: the number of fibers per muscle. Each fiber represents one functional group. The fiber count simultaneously determines:
1. Physics accuracy (quadrature resolution)
2. Action space size (one activation per fiber)
3. Smoothness (implicitly, via spatial density — more fibers → smaller groups → less activation jump expected between neighbors)
4. Functional grouping (each fiber = one group)

No separate smoothness penalty, control-point parameterization, or post-hoc clustering needed. The LOD hierarchy lets you vary fiber count, and everything else follows.

**Inter-muscle coupling**: Boundary fibers of adjacent muscles (e.g., gluteus minimus–medius border) could also be grouped by moment arm similarity, preventing the policy from zeroing out small muscles while neighbors are highly active.

## Contour Resolution: Absorbed by Fiber LOD

Current approach: heuristic cherry-picking of contour levels based on original-refined error comparison. Baseline paper also treats contour count as a tunable parameter.

**Initially considered making contour resolution adaptive or oversampled**, but this is impractical: the soft body tet simulation is built from the contour-derived mesh, and waypoint positions come from that simulation. Changing contour resolution means re-running the full tet sim pipeline. Running separate sims per resolution is infeasible. Extracting contours from a deformed original mesh can't guarantee bounding plane / contour correspondence is maintained.

**Decision: fix contour resolution.** Use the current heuristic (error-based cherry-picking), treat it as fixed infrastructure. The multi-fiber LOD hierarchy operates downstream — varying fiber count and activation grouping within a fixed volume representation. One fewer moving part.

**Contour spacing has hard constraints in both directions:**
- **Lower bound (collision safety)**: Vertex-only collision detection means long edges between sparse contours can penetrate each other undetected. Max safe spacing ≈ minimum cross-section diameter at that region — ensures no edge is longer than the muscle is wide.
- **Upper bound (runtime)**: Each contour contributes waypoints to the line muscle model. Too many waypoints → too slow for real-time simulation.
- Where muscle is thin (near tendons) → contours must be closer. Where thick (belly) → can be sparser.
- This is a one-time-per-muscle decision but must be done carefully since simulation correctness (not just quality) depends on it.

**Current contour selection rule**: Greedy variant of Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm applied to the muscle centerline. Uses centroid interpolation error with relative threshold (0.5% of muscle length). Limitation: centroid-only misses cross-section shape changes, tapering, and twisting.

**Proposed improvement — 3D inertia tensor RDP**:
- Replace centroid error with 3D inertia tensor interpolation error. The 3×3 inertia tensor computed from contour points' 3D coordinates captures: cross-sectional shape, size, aspect ratio, plane orientation, AND curvature — all in one matrix.
- Error metric: relative Frobenius norm `||I_actual - I_interp||_F / ||I_actual||_F`
- Threshold: single universal value (~5%, tuned visually on a few representative muscles)
- Absolute (scale-independent via relative error) — small muscles naturally get fewer contours
- Greedy (not recursive) — handles merged/non-merged stream region constraints naturally
- Builds piecewise-linear approximation of the centerline, greedily adding points where the approximation error is worst. Shape variation naturally determines density — more contours at curvature/cross-section changes, fewer at straight uniform sections.

## Practical Fiber Grouping Method

Full ROM moment arm computation for every fiber is combinatorially expensive. Cheaper proxies that correlate strongly with mechanical similarity:

1. **Attachment-point clustering (primary)**: Fibers with nearby origin and insertion points have similar moment arms. Just cluster fiber endpoint coordinates — instant, no simulation needed.
2. **Single-pose moment arm check (validation)**: For muscles with complex wrapping, compute moment arms at neutral pose only. Catches cases where nearby fibers wrap differently around bone.
3. **Full ROM sweep (only for publication)**: Quantitative error bounds, not needed for grouping.

Most muscles: endpoint clustering alone is sufficient. Complex wrapping muscles: add the single-pose check.

## Resolution-Adaptive Model (LOD for Muscles)

**Goal**: A single model where fiber count is adjustable per muscle without changing functionality. More fibers = more accurate but slower, fewer = coarser but faster. Same activation input → approximately same joint torque at any resolution.

**Key insight**: The scalar field is the ground truth model. Fibers are quadrature points sampling it. Activation field (control points) is independent of quadrature resolution.

**Approach — hierarchical clustering**:
1. Oversample each muscle with dense fibers from the scalar field
2. Compute moment arm profiles across full joint ROM for each fiber
3. Hierarchical clustering by moment arm similarity
4. Cut the tree at any level to get desired fiber count per muscle

Each representative fiber at a coarser level carries aggregated properties:
- Position: cluster centroid in scalar field
- Max force: sum of children's max forces (preserves PCSA)
- Moment arm: area-weighted average of children's curves

**Force consistency** holds when: activation field is smooth (ensured by control points) and within-cluster moment arms are similar (ensured by clustering criterion).

**Practical benefits**:
- Training: low-res for RL exploration, high-res for refinement
- Deployment: real-time → low-res, offline analysis → high-res
- Validation: compare torque across resolutions for error bounds

## Structural Lower Bounds on Fiber Count

Some muscles have hard minimum fiber counts — going below loses qualitative correctness, not just accuracy:

- **Multiple origins/insertions**: one fiber per attachment site minimum (biceps ≥2, triceps ≥3, quadriceps ≥4)
- **Moment arm sign change**: fibers producing opposite-direction torque cannot average (deltoid ≥2–3)
- **Multi-joint crossing**: fibers crossing different joint subsets can never merge (gastrocnemius vs soleus)

The hierarchical clustering naturally respects these — maximally dissimilar profiles never cluster together. The hierarchy output per muscle has a structural floor that the global accuracy budget distributes fibers above.

## Pennation Angles

- Currently not modeled because tendons are not implemented in the simulation
- With tendons, multi-fiber naturally captures pennation: fiber paths from scalar field come out angled relative to tendon direction, and effective force = F × cos(angle)
- Dynamic pennation (angle changes with contraction) would emerge from volume deformation — pennation becomes a geometric consequence, not a parameter
- **Without tendons**: a static cos(pennation_angle) scaling on max force per muscle is a minimal correction using literature values
- **Priority ordering**: tendon elasticity (high impact on RL gait energetics) > pennation scaling > dynamic pennation > full aponeurosis modeling

## Validation Plan

To prove multi-fiber physical correctness without RL:
1. Pick muscles where single-fiber is most suspect (gluteus maximus, deltoid)
2. Compare joint torque at uniform activation across ROM: single-fiber vs multi-fiber vs dense ground truth
3. If multi-fiber matches ground truth while single-fiber diverges → physical benefit demonstrated
